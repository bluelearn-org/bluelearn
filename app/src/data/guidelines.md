This guide serves as a "quick" manual for creating content on Bluelearn. Adhering to these constraints is critical; publishing non-compliant guides will hurt the presentation and make maintenance more difficult for the Knowledge Graph.

## 1. Core Principles

- **Atomic Content**: Guides must explain one (and only one) **Primary Topic**. Knowledge dumps (long guides explaining multiple topics) must be split ("atomized") into multiple atomic guides.
- **Narrative Tension**: Learning requires tension. Follow a progression of problem, struggle, and resolution. Do not put the conclusion first, as doing so increases the chance that the reader will discard the explanatory mechanism.
- **Explanatory Focus**: Writers must dismantle their own assumptions and explain ideas step-by-step to newcomers. Repeat important concepts, but avoid over-repeating less important ones.
- **Quality**: Guides must contain no spelling errors, be grammatically sound, and be easy to understand.

## 2. Content & Scope

- **Primary Topic:** This is the unique idea the guide centers around. It must be explicitly stated in the title and description. _If you're confused as to what a Primary Topic is, it's essentially the Guide's Title. If the Title is "How to flip a pancake" then that is the Primary Topic of the guide. Everything in the guide should directly address that topic. If the "How to flip a pancake" guide also teaches you how to cook an egg, that explanation would **not** be part of the Primary Topic, and is beyond the scope of the guide. "How to cook an egg" should instead be made into a separate guide._
- **Secondary Topic:** A Secondary Topic is a topic which is used to help explain a Primary Topic, but also has it's own nuances and complexities. If you want to explain a Secondary Topic in detail, _it must be detailed in a separate Guide/Prerequisite._ Explanations of Secondary Topics must always be brief & to-the-point. Guides which over-explain Secondary Topics are considered knowledge dumps, and should be rejected.
- **Prerequisites & Connections**: Guides must flow naturally from their prerequisite guides and used to explain the current topic. A Guide must not contradict it's prerequisites. When connections are drawn between guides, the relationship should be mentioned in one or both guide's text.
- **Introducing New Concepts (Jargon)**: _Jargon is specialized or technical vocabulary, specific to a culture or sub-culture, which has a different - or no - meaning outside that culture._ Jargon should either be defined inside the guide where it's introduced, or in a prerequisite guide. Jargon should be treated similarly to Secondary Topics; meaning it should only be introduced and explained if it's essential to the Primary Topic. If you wish to explain the jargon in more detail, it must be done in a separate guide.
    - Minimize jargon whenever possible.
    - When Jargon is introduced, a link must be placed on your guide, so the jargon maps directly to it's definition guide. (A to-do prerequisite, added to a guide, counts as a link)
- **Legal Compliance**: Guides must not contain copyrighted or illegal content.
- **Disclaimers**: Guides with medical content must always contain at the top of the guide "Disclaimer: This article is for informational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider for diagnosis and treatment recommendations."

## 3. Writing Style

- **Voice**: Both _Third-Person Voice_ (e.g. "He will then flip the Pancake") and _active, Second-Person Voice_ (e.g., "You will then flip the pancake") are acceptable. **First-Person Voice is not allowed** (e.g. "I will flip the pancake"), except in examples and analogies.
- **Analogies & Metaphors**: Provide culturally neutral, tangible analogies for abstract ideas. Avoid culturally specific idioms or sports metaphors (e.g., "slam dunk", "home run"). Default to universal, physics-based metaphors (e.g., "push", "pull", "jump").
- **Story-Driven Content:** Stories must teach an idea/concept. The moral of the story, or concept that the story is meant to teach, should be explicitly defined at the end of the guide. Pure fiction and non-fiction aren't allowed.
- **Word Count**: Guides should usually be kept under 2000 words. Only exceed if topic structurally demands it.
- **Paragraph Length**: Paragraphs must generally be 5 sentences or less.
- **Sentence Length:** Sentences can be any length, but must be as concise as possible. Readers must be able to identify, on a single pass, what the sentence is about and what it asserts.
- **Labels of Ease:** Unearned reassurances like "This is easy" or "Any beginner can do this" are prohibited.
- **Audience Declaration:** An Audience Declaration is required on base guides (guides with no prerequisites), and when there's an existing guide with a similar topic. Declarations should be made at the beginning of the guide, and specific. Example: "This guide is for intermediate Python programmers who have completed the 'Introduction to Python' and 'Data Structures in Python' guides."

## 4. Formatting & Assets

- **Title**: Must be declarative (stating exactly what the guide is about).
- **Summary**: Provide a brief 1-3 sentence summary (also known as a "description") which states what the guide will explain, when it will be applied or useful, or what other guides it connects to.
- **Headings**: Use headings 2-6 to characterize sections within the guide. Heading 1 is reserved for the guide title.
- **Emphasis**: Use _italics_ for general emphasis. Do not use italics for introducing minor concepts (use **boldface** instead).
- **Callouts:** Limit to one callout per chunk of content. Generally keep callout length 1-4 sentences.  
    
    |Callout Type|Meaning|Use When|
    |---|---|---|
    |**Info**|Neutral context|Extra non-essential understanding helps.|
    |**Note**|Important clarification|Reader needs to know this to avoid confusion.|
    |**Tip**|Optional improvement|Nice to know, but not required for the core task.|
    |**Caution**|Possible bad outcome|An action could ruin the result or lead to a minor failure.|
    |**Warning**|Possible major damage|Real risk of serious failure, data loss, or harm.|
    |**Danger**|Immediate severe harm|Life-threatening or permanently damaging. Use extremely rarely.|
    
- **Images**: Images are allowed to bolster a guide but must be complementary and placed at appropriate locations (e.g., in a recipe guide, images should correspond to the appropriate steps).
    - Allowed formats: `.jpg`, `.png`, `.webp`.
    - Max file size: 10MB.
    - **IMAGES WITH TRANSPARENCY WILL NOT DISPLAY PROPERLY.** Please make sure you do not use images with transparent backgrounds.
