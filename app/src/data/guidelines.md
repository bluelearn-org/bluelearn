This guide serves as a "quick" manual for creating content on Bluelearn (view full guidelines here: [https://app.bluelearn.org/browse?q=bluelearn+content+standards](<https://app.bluelearn.org/browse?q=bluelearn+content+standards>)). Adhering to these constraints is critical; publishing non-compliant guides will hurt the presentation and make maintenance more difficult for the Knowledge Graph.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) Content Policy

Writers are free to use AI to *assist with research and drafting*, but the final guide must be written in the author's own words, must use original phrasing/verbiage, original Titles & Headings, original examples & analogies, and must eliminate common AI conventions (such as em dashes "—"). Content which *appears* to be AI generated will be rejected, and changes will be requested. Accounts that consistently submit this kind of content may be suspended or banned.

## 1. Core Principles

* **Atomic Content**: Guides must explain one (and only one) **Primary Topic**. Knowledge dumps (long guides explaining multiple topics) must be split ("atomized") into multiple atomic guides.
* **Narrative Tension**: Learning requires tension. Follow a progression of problem, struggle, and resolution. Do not put the conclusion first.
* **Explanatory Focus**: Writers must dismantle their own assumptions and explain ideas step-by-step to newcomers. Repeat important concepts, but avoid over-repeating less important ones.
* **Quality**: Guides must contain no spelling errors, be grammatically sound, and be easy to understand.

## 2. Content & Scope

* **Primary Topic:** This is the unique idea the guide centers around. It must be explicitly stated in the title and description.  If you're confused as to what a Primary Topic is, it's essentially the Guide's Title.  *If the Title is "How to flip a pancake" then that is the Primary Topic of the guide. Content should center around that.*
* **Secondary Topic:**  A Secondary Topic is a topic which is used to help explain a Primary Topic.  If you want to explain a Secondary Topic in detail, *it must be detailed in a separate Guide/Prerequisite.*
* **Prerequisites & Connections**: Guides must flow naturally from their prerequisite guides and used to explain the current topic. A Guide must not contradict it's prerequisites.
* **Introducing New Concepts (Jargon)**: *Jargon is specialized or technical vocabulary, specific to a culture or sub-culture, which has different (or no) meaning outside that culture.*  Jargon should either be defined inside the guide where it's introduced, or linked to a prerequisite guide.  Minimize jargon when possible.
* **Legal Compliance**: Guides must not contain copyrighted or illegal material.
* **Disclaimers**: Appropriate Disclaimers must be added to guides which contain Medical, Financial, Legal, Mature, Profane, or Potentially Dangerous content.

## 3. Writing Style

* **Word Count**: Guides should usually be kept under 2000 words. Only exceed if topic structurally demands it.
* **Paragraph Length**: Paragraphs must generally be 5 sentences or less.
* **Sentence Length:** Sentences can be any length, but must be as concise as possible.
* **Labels of Ease:** Unearned reassurances like "This is easy" or "Any beginner can do this" are not allowed.
* **Voice**: *Third-Person Voice* (e.g. "He will jump") and *active, Second-Person Voice* (e.g., "You will jump") are allowed. **Avoid First Person Voice** (e.g. "I will jump"), except in examples and analogies.
* **Analogies & Metaphors**: Avoid culturally specific idioms (e.g., "slam dunk", "home run"). Default to more universal terminologies (e.g., "push", "pull", "jump").
* **Story-Driven Content:** Stories must teach an idea/concept. The moral or point of the story should be explicitly stated at the end. Pure fiction and non-fiction aren't allowed.
* **Audience Declaration:** A specific Audience Declaration is required on guides with no prerequisites, at the beginning of the guide. Example: "This guide is for intermediate Python programmers who have completed the 'Introduction to Python' guide."

## 4. Formatting & Assets

* **Title**: Must be declarative (stating exactly what the guide is about).
* **Summary**: Provide a brief 1-3 sentence summary (also known as a "description") which states what the guide will explain, when it will be applied or useful, or what other guides it connects to.
* **Headings**: Use headings 2-6 to characterize sections within the guide. Heading 1 is reserved for the guide title.
* **Emphasis**: Use *italics* for general emphasis. Do not use italics for introducing minor concepts (use **boldface** instead).
* **Callouts:** Limit to one callout per chunk of content. Generally keep callout length 1-4 sentences.

  | Callout Type | Meaning | Use When |
  | -- | -- | -- |
  | **Info** | Neutral context | Extra non-essential understanding helps. |
  | **Note** | Important clarification | Reader needs to know this to avoid confusion. |
  | **Tip** | Optional improvement | Nice to know, but not required for the core task. |
  | **Caution** | Possible bad outcome | An action could ruin the result or lead to a minor failure. |
  | **Warning** | Possible major damage | Real risk of serious failure, data loss, or harm. |
  | **Danger** | Immediate severe harm | Life-threatening or permanently damaging. Use extremely rarely. |
* **Images**: Images are allowed to bolster a guide but must be complementary and placed at appropriate locations (e.g., in a recipe guide, images should correspond to the appropriate steps).
  * Allowed formats: `.jpg`, `.png`, `.webp`.
  * Max file size: 10MB.
  * **IMAGES WITH TRANSPARENCY WILL NOT DISPLAY PROPERLY.** Please make sure you do not use images with transparent backgrounds.
